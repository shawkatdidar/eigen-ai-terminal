## Today I Learned

### MFU: why "more GPUs" and "more output" are not the same question

**Why this came up:** Anjney Midha's claim that a frontier lab might run at **sub-10% MFU** while best-in-class is 60–70% reframes the entire compute-scarcity narrative — and only makes sense if you have the mental model for what MFU is.

**The concept — Model FLOPs Utilization:**

A GPU has a theoretical peak throughput (FLOPs/second). During a training run, the *useful* math (the forward/backward passes the model actually needs) almost never runs at that peak, because the chip spends time waiting:
- **moving data** between HBM and compute cores (memory-bandwidth bound),
- **communicating** between GPUs (network-bound, in distributed training),
- **stalling** on scheduling, pipeline bubbles, and synchronization.

**MFU = (useful FLOPs actually performed) / (theoretical peak FLOPs the hardware could have performed)** over the same wall-clock time. An MFU of 40% means 60% of the hardware you paid for produced nothing.

**Why the framework changes a decision:**

The naive model of AI scaling is "capability ∝ compute, and compute ∝ number of GPUs you can buy." MFU breaks that into two independent levers:

1. **How many GPUs you have** (capex — what everyone talks about).
2. **What fraction of them you actually use** (MFU — systems engineering).

A lab at sub-10% MFU has a **~6–7× throughput gain available without buying a single additional GPU** — purely from utilization engineering (better kernels, overlap of compute and communication, parallelism strategy, removing pipeline bubbles). That is why the same headline FLOPs can produce wildly different amounts of trained model depending on who runs the cluster.

**The judgment payoff:** When you read "Lab X has N GPUs" or "Company Y spent $Z on compute," that number is an *upper bound* on what they can produce, not a measure of it. The real question is **N × MFU**. A smaller cluster at 60% MFU can out-train a larger cluster at 10%. This is why "outputmaxxing" (extracting more from installed hardware) is a distinct strategic lever from "buy more hardware" — and why a systems team that can hold high MFU is a genuine, non-obvious moat.

**Where this connects:** It's the training-side analog of the inference-side cost-collapse thesis (CONV-002) and the agent-cost work (TokenPilot, June 15): in all three, the lever is *efficiency of using hardware you already have*, not acquiring more. The compute-scarcity story is real on the capex axis but partly an artifact of low utilization on the throughput axis.
