## Today I Learned

### Why open-weight models track the frontier at a roughly *fixed* lag (and what that implies)

**Why this came up:** Dwarkesh Patel's claim that AI progress is **data-limited, not sample-efficiency-limited**, plus the cited Epoch figure that **open models lag the frontier by ~4 months**, together form a mental model that changes how you forecast the open-vs-closed race (and how you read GLM-5.2 #2 and PRED-014).

**The mechanism — three linked claims:**

1. **Progress comes from widening the data distribution, not from models learning more per example.** Frontier models train on tens-to-hundreds of *trillions* of tokens; a human sees ~200M in a lifetime and is far more capable per token. We have not closed that sample-efficiency gap — we have brute-forced around it with more and better data. RL/GRPO is, in this framing, *not* a new learning paradigm but **synthetic data generation around a verifier**: spend compute generating many rollouts, keep the ones a verifier scores as correct, and train on those. RL is "find good data with compute," then ordinary next-token training on that data.

2. **If progress is data-driven, the question becomes: how transferable is the data?** Here's the key asymmetry Dwarkesh names: **data distills from public APIs more easily than architectural tricks or training optimizations do.** You can query a frontier model at scale, capture its outputs, and train an open model to imitate the resulting distribution — without ever knowing the frontier lab's kernels, parallelism strategy, or data-curation secrets. Capabilities leak through the output channel; implementation moats don't.

3. **Therefore the lag is roughly *fixed*, not widening.** Because the dominant input (data/distribution) is partially observable and distillable, open models track the frontier at a roughly constant offset (~4 months per Epoch) rather than falling progressively further behind. The frontier's moat is *time*, not *secrecy*.

**The judgment payoff — what this lets you predict without asking an AI:**

- When you see "open model X matches closed model Y," don't ask "did they steal the architecture?" Ask "what frontier outputs were available to distill, and when?" The capability transfer runs through *data access*, not leaked weights.
- A *fixed* lag has a sharp implication for **PRED-014** (open model reaches outright #1): a constant ~1-quarter offset means open models get *very close* (GLM-5.2 at #2) but only reach #1 in the windows when the frontier *pauses between releases*. The bet isn't "open catches up" — it's "open catches a gap in the frontier's release cadence."
- It reframes what a frontier lab's moat actually is. If implementation tricks don't transfer but data/distribution does, the durable moats are the things that **don't leak through outputs**: proprietary data the model was trained on (not its outputs), real-time data flywheels, and distribution/integration — not the training recipe.

**The caution:** this is informed analysis from one source (Dwarkesh) citing one aggregator (Epoch), not a primary measurement. The "~4 months" is a useful order-of-magnitude, not a constant of nature. But the *mechanism* (data distills, tricks don't) is the durable part of the model.

**Where this connects:** It's the demand-side mirror of yesterday's MFU lesson. MFU said "installed compute is underused — efficiency is a hidden lever." This says "the frontier's lead is data-distance, and data is partly observable — so the lead is a *time* lead, not a *secrecy* lead." Both cut against the naive "more GPUs + secret sauce = permanent moat" story.

### Quick Glossary

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Sample efficiency | How much a model learns *per training example*; humans are thousands-to-millions× more sample-efficient than current LLMs (which compensate with vastly more data) |
| RL as synthetic data generation | Framing RL/GRPO as "spend compute to generate many rollouts, keep the verifier-approved ones, train on them" — i.e., a data-creation process, not a distinct learning paradigm |
| Distillation from public APIs | Capturing a frontier model's outputs at scale to train an open model toward its capability distribution — the channel through which capabilities leak even when implementation doesn't |
| Fixed-lag dynamics | Open models tracking the frontier at a roughly constant time offset (~4 months, per Epoch) rather than diverging — because the dominant input (data) is partially observable |
| Ultrasound-on-chip | Solid-state ultrasound using arrays of MEMS/piezoelectric transducers with no moving parts (cf. Butterfly Network) — the approach behind the Midjourney Scanner |
| Evidence tier (medical AI) | The strength of the source behind a clinical claim: peer-reviewed venue (NEJM AI) > product announcement; the axis on which Midjourney's launch and OpenAI's rare-disease study diverge |
